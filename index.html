01 Introduction

I came to data science from computer engineering. The question that pulled me in was a simple one, how does a model go from a table of numbers to a decision that holds up in the real world? Most of what I've built since has been an attempt to answer it properly.

My work spans machine learning, deep learning, and the unglamorous plumbing underneath: cleaning, feature engineering, and pipelines that don't fall over. I've trained a GAN to generate anime artwork, built predictive models on messy datasets, and written automation that scrapes and structures data other people would rather not touch.

Today I'm completing an MSc in Data Science at Coventry University while building production-minded ML projects. Alongside that I've solved 300+ algorithm problems and placed fourth in a machine learning hackathon.

The gap between a good model and a useful one is engineering. That gap is where I like to work.

GAN for anime generation

Problem. Generating believable anime artwork means a model has to learn structure and style at once — and GANs are notorious for collapsing before they get there.

Approach. A generative adversarial network with a custom architecture in TensorFlow and Keras, tuned for image quality over training speed and stabilised against mode collapse.

Code ↗

Problem. Real datasets rarely arrive clean. Pulling reliable signal out of them takes more than dropping a model on top of the raw table.

Approach. An end-to-end pipeline comparing multiple algorithms, with feature engineering doing most of the heavy lifting and honest evaluation deciding what actually shipped.
